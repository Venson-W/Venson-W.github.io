My research connects numerical methods with the physics of fault slip, from individual grains and thin gouge layers to fault-scale rupture and fluid migration.

01 · Numerical methods and rupture dynamics

Hybrid Numerical Schemes Development for Rupture Dynamics

Earthquake rupture links large-scale fault slip to deformation within gouge layers only millimeters thick. I develop hybrid numerical schemes that bridge these scales by coupling a spectral boundary integral method for the surrounding poroelastic rock with staggered-grid finite differences inside the fault gouge. The framework resolves interacting heat, fluid-pressure, and deformation processes, including thermal pressurization and the emergence of narrow shear zones. Simulations show that the poroelastic response of the host rock can strongly influence how localized slip migrates across the gouge, with implications for interpreting geological records of past earthquakes. For the rapid rupture examined in this study, an undrained approximation captures much of the bulk response.

Wang, Y.-H. and Heimisson, E. R. (2026). Poroelastic Bulk Controls on Shear Localization in Fault Gouge During Earthquake Rupture: Insights From a Hybrid Numerical Framework. Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth, 131, e2025JB032291. DOI: 10.1029/2025JB032291.

02 · Fluid-induced fault slip

Cross-scale Modelling of Fluid-induced Fault Slip

I investigate how fluid migration and pore-pressure changes influence fault slip across scales. This work connects the mechanics of individual grains and granular fault gouge with the larger-scale response of a fluid-infiltrated fault. By combining grain-scale modelling with efficient continuum and hybrid numerical approaches, I aim to understand how local rearrangements, frictional evolution, and fluid-solid interactions contribute to the onset and development of slip. The goal is to link small-scale physical processes to fault-scale behaviour and improve our understanding of fluid-induced seismicity.
